# Skyclave environment reference

`skyclave-arena-v2.png` is a generated environment concept, not an in-game screenshot. It was produced with the built-in image generation tool on 2026-10-11. The material source images and exact prompts are in `../textures/generated-source/`.

The implementation targets larger 48m room cells, a continuous ground floor and one upper gallery at 3.5m, opposing stair routes, readable projectile lanes, pillars and off-axis jumpable cover. Only the dedicated abyss room removes large sections of floor. Actual gameplay geometry, navigation and collisions remain the source of truth; a concept image does not define another playable floor.

The setting follows the [official Zendikar plane description](https://magic.wizards.com/en/story/zendikar-plane) and [Skyclave account](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/feature/zendikar-things-have-changed-2020-09-01): volatile terrain, floating ruins and ancient carved monoliths. Room interiors and ornaments are this game's designs. [BPM's developer interview](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/building-ai-to-fight-on-the-beat-of-bpm-bullets-per-minute) informs modular room circulation, combat variety and the rhythm of movement, rather than establishing exact real-world room dimensions.

First-person camera at 1.7m with wide 80 degree view inside a much larger 48m square ancient floating Skyclave ruin arena. The design language is Zendikar: angular Kor architecture, eroded diamond and hedron engravings, tilted monolithic basalt piers, carefully bevelled asymmetric volcanic stone slabs, ancient bronze inlays, moss and wet mineral streaks, small embedded turquoise mana veins and warm lamps. Above the arena hang huge fractured triangular stone fragments and a few aged hedrons, skylight shows distant floating ruins, but the playable space is an enclosed ruin. Obsidian and dusty gray stone, warm aged bronze, subtle turquoise accents. Natural detailed PBR textures, restrained bloom, realistic darkness and baked-looking pillar shadows, pockets of readable amber and turquoise light, visible surface normals and stone wear. Ordinary floor in this main arena is continuous with no random pits. At the far end a dim translucent cyan portal sits against a nearly black backing inside an angular stone doorway. One broad staircase dominates the right foreground, an opposing stair is visible across the room, upper walkway visibly connects both so multiplayer circulation is clear. Threat silhouettes would remain readable without glowing bodies. No Gothic cathedral ornament, no religious stained glass imagery, no Norse motifs, no neon plastic, no cartoon look, no boxy placeholder meshes. Concrete buildable modular architecture, not impossible Escher structures. Show beautiful rich geometry and materials, ground-level eye perspective that clearly demonstrates the large room and both floor levels.
